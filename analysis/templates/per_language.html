Workload Distribution Ridgeline

Each ridge below shows the full timing distribution (KDE) at a given workload level, with the largest workload (1 M rolls) at the bottom. A dot on each baseline marks the mean, providing another way to visually see how the language scales up as the workload increases. Hover anywhere on a ridge for summary statistics (mean, std, CV). Distributions that narrow and shift left as workloads decrease indicate stable, predictable scaling.

Trial Distribution by Workload

Select a workload below to examine the histogram of individual trial times. A tight, symmetric histogram indicates stable timing. A long right tail implies occasional outlier runs — likely from OS scheduling preemptions or, in Java’s case, JIT compilation events. Along the x-axis, individual run times are marked as a rug plot to show how the raw data translates into the histogram, as well as visually inspect for interesting clusters.

Trial Sequence by Workload

Each point in this chart represents one timed trial plotted in the order it was executed within its batch run. Faint lines show individual batch runs; the bold line is the cross-batch mean at each trial position. A mean line that slopes downward from left to right is the hallmark of a warm-up effect: the runtime gets faster once CPU caches and (for Java) the JIT compiler have optimised the hot path. Slow trials scattered randomly across positions instead point to OS scheduling noise rather than warm-up.
